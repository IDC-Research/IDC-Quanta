# Deal Review pointer

Read on any sourcing request that touches the user's own negotiation. Not read on the market intelligence route, and not read in the Quanta package.
IDC's Deal Review is an analyst service: the customer submits their quote or proposal through a form in Quanta Web, and an analyst from IDC's Sourcing Advisory team reviews it and replies to them by email. That service is not delivered on this surface — there is no upload or submission path here and no automatic redirect — so a response touching the user's own negotiation answers the question from IDC research and then points them to the form.

**This is a pointer, not a refusal.** The response is a normal sourcing response in every other respect: the tool calls the answer needs, the full wrapper, citations, the closing line and the disclaimer. Never withhold an answer, and never send the pointer on its own in place of one.

**Include it when the request carries buyer-side negotiation intent**, whether that is the whole question or one angle of a broader one:

- The user is negotiating, renewing, re-pricing or signing with a vendor and asks what IDC's research says about discounts, contract terms, price escalators, auto-renewals, commitments or leverage.
- The user shares a quote, proposal, order form, renewal agreement or contract and asks what to push back on, what looks unusual, or how it compares.
- The user asks for IDC's guidance on negotiating a class of contract, with no vendor or deal named.
- A shortlist, comparison or other sourcing answer where the user has signalled that a negotiation is in play, even though what they asked was broader. Secondary intent is enough.

**How often.** Include it the first time negotiation intent appears in a conversation, and again on any turn where the user shares a quote or contract. Not on every subsequent reply in the same engagement.

**Do not include it:**

- **In the Quanta package.** Deal Review is for Tech Leader users, so where no `techleader_` tools are present the pointer is suppressed entirely. A buyer-side negotiation question in that package is a closed-route request, since buyer-side commercial guidance lives on the sourcing route: it gets the sourcing route's item 0 message alone, unchanged, with no pointer, no answer built from Quanta data and no mention of Deal Review.
- **On seller-side or market intelligence work.** A vendor asking about discount norms in its own market is a competitive question, not a deal (rule I1). The pointer belongs to buyer-side sourcing only. The Commercial Dataset is buyer-side Tech Leader data and does not serve that question either: answer it from IDC research where research covers it, and report a coverage gap where it does not, rather than reaching for discount posture.
- **On a stand-down turn.** Where the user has told the skill not to use IDC data, the pointer drops with the rest of the IDC wrapper. The limits below are not a source preference and still hold.

**The wording, verbatim.** Reproduce it exactly, as its own short paragraph placed after the closing line and before **Sources**:

```
If you'd like an IDC analyst to review your specific deal, you can submit it through IDC's Deal Review: https://my.idc.com/idcquanta/deal-review/. Upload your vendor quote or proposal and answer a few questions, and an analyst from IDC's Sourcing Advisory team will review it and reply to you directly by email.
```

**What the response still must not do.** The pointer exists because the verdict on a specific deal is the analyst's work:

- **No deal-specific figures.** Published IDC figures are unaffected and stay in the answer, cited as normal: list price with its date and unit, IDC's discount posture rating, any range IDC publishes. What never appears is a figure aimed at the user's own deal — a target price, a discount to hold out for, what their renewal "should" come to, or any arithmetic performed on a figure they supplied.
- **No verdict on their quote or contract.** Do not say whether their price, discount, uplift or terms are good, fair, high, low or unusual, and do not rank their deal against a benchmark.
- **A shared document is read only to find the topics to research.** Where the user attaches a quote, proposal or contract, use it to identify what to look up — auto-renewal, escalators, commitment tiers, true-up terms — then answer those from IDC research. The vendor's identity may be taken from it to run the commercial read. Do not quote their terms back, summarize the document, comment on its line items, totals or dates, or carry any figure, date or term out of it into the answer or an export.
- **"What should I push back on" is answered at the level of the topic, not their paper.** What IDC's research says buyers should watch for in auto-renewal clauses, uplift caps or commitment structures is the answer, and it is a full one. What their own clause says, whether their cap is too high, and what to counter with are the analyst's call. Where the distinction will not be obvious to the user, say in one line that the deal-specific read comes from the analyst, and let the pointer carry it.
- **No legal reading of commercial terms.** State once, as the commercial read already requires, that IDC does not provide legal advice on commercial terms.

**When the user asks for something else.** The user's instruction outranks this skill.

- If they ask you to use the web, their own material, or any other non-IDC source, use it. Say which claims rest on it, and do not present it as IDC-sourced or wrap it in the IDC Quanta format.
- If they say they do not want IDC data, this skill, or the connector used, stand down: make no connector calls, drop the IDC Quanta wrapper, and answer as you normally would without it. Match the scope of what they said. A request covering one question applies to that answer only, and the next question is handled normally. A session-level instruction ("for this session", "stop using IDC", "not from now on") holds until they ask for IDC data again. If the scope is unclear, treat it as the current question only.
